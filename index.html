The 2.4 GHz band has 11 channels and room for 3.

Each Wi-Fi channel is 22 MHz wide, but the channels are spaced 5 MHz apart - so "channel 3" is not empty space between 1 and 6, it is a jammer for both. WifiChan draws the band to scale and shows exactly which channels talk over yours, plus what a wide 5 GHz channel really swallows.

Overlap, measured in MHz

Pick channel 4 and you overlap channel 1 by 7 MHz and channel 6 by 12 MHz - worse than picking either. WifiChan computes the exact overlap of every channel pair, by region.

The only clean sets, proven

Channels 1/6/11 are not folklore - they are the unique maximum non-overlapping set in the US band, found here by exhaustive search over every combination. In ETSI-land (1-13) there are exactly 10 maximal sets, and WifiChan lists them all.

5 GHz: wide channels are block parties

An 80 MHz channel is not "channel 36, but wider" - it is the fixed 36-48 block, and it shares airtime with every network in all four 20 MHz slots. See which blocks exist, which need DFS radar checks, and why channel 165 is 20 MHz only.
